1Executive Summary
Three ideas that shaped the entire plan

PayMe isn't a payments company — it's an Embedded Finance platform

The real customer isn't the small business. It's the platform that serves small businesses. The partner embeds PayMe's services into their own product — and the small business uses them as a natural part of their workflow.

Two engines, not one: Outbound + Inbound

Instead of relying only on new partnerships, I built a model that also leverages existing partners (Wolt, Wix, Isracard) to add more products. If one engine stalls, the other picks up the slack.

Real value — not just "here's your commission"

Every partner gets a tailored reason to move: 10bis gets courier retention. Arbox gets a feature their users keep asking for. Plannie doubles their ARPU. Wix fixes a broken user experience. Not a generic revenue share pitch.

3Inbound Strategy: Pain vs. Hunger
Two types of opportunities — each requires a different approach

🔥 Pain

A problem the partner is already dealing with

Wix: Israeli merchants juggle 3 separate systems — Wix for the site, PayMe/Isracard for payments, iCount for invoicing. Broken experience.

Zester: Restaurants pay suppliers through PayMe but manage invoices elsewhere. Two systems instead of one.

🍽️ Hunger

An opportunity the partner wants to grab

Wolt Merchants: Adding Accounting for restaurants = new revenue stream + stronger lock-in with merchant partners.

Isracard/Cal: Adding Accounting + Promoter = standing out from every other payment provider.

5Value Proposition — Beyond Commission
What does the partner actually gain, besides revenue share?

10bis

❌ Not commission
✅ Courier retention — a courier who can't handle paperwork quits. PayMe reduces churn. Plus, it replaces Keycost (99 ILS/mo) with a better service — no added cost.

Arbox

❌ Not commission
✅ A feature their users keep asking for — "Why don't you have bookkeeping?" PayMe gives them that feature without writing a single line of code.

Plannie

❌ Not commission
✅ Doubling their ARPU — from 50 ILS/mo (scheduling) to 200 ILS/mo (scheduling + payments + invoicing). PayMe lets Plannie charge more.

Wix

❌ Not commission
✅ A complete user experience — Israeli merchants currently manage 3 separate systems. Adding Accounting means everything in one place. Lock-in + new revenue stream.

8My Honest Take
What I actually believe about this plan

Month 1 is for planning, not revenue

Anyone who promises revenue in month one doesn't understand how partnerships work. Month 1 is research, outreach, and building the foundation.

Start with the inner circle

Inbound first — because there's already trust, existing integrations, and short sales cycles. Quick wins build momentum. New partnerships run in parallel.

The spreadsheet is a model, not a prophecy

The numbers are grounded estimates, not guarantees. They need to be validated against reality every single week. That's how you actually hit targets — by adjusting, not by hoping.

Month 3 = momentum. Month 4 = closings.

Deals that were seeded in month 2 should start showing signs of closing in month 3. By month 4, the pipeline converts to real MRR.
